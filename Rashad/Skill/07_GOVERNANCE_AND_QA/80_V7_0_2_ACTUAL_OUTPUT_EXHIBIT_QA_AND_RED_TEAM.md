## Principle
Passing the QA framework's own tests does **not** mean a specific rendered deck passed visual QA. The current user-visible authority is `07_GOVERNANCE_AND_QA/81_V7_1_ACTUAL_PIXEL_QA_CLOSED_LOOP_AND_GOLDEN_ACCEPTANCE.md`.

## Page-level actual-output QA foundation
Evaluate actual production pixels for:
- 5-second comprehension;
- answer-first clarity;
- visual-form fitness;
- simplicity / restraint;
- executive hierarchy;
- evidence density and source binding;
- artifact usefulness versus decoration;
- generic-layout risk;
- diagram necessity;
- whitespace quality and information economy;
- Arabic/RTL physical reading path when applicable;
- brand fidelity, typography and production integrity.

If the page becomes stronger after removing the diagram, the diagram fails.

## Deck-level actual-output QA foundation
Review the actual rendered deck/montage for diagram overuse, repeated communication strategy, repeated structural fingerprint, positional winner bias, reused render hashes, lack of calm/minimal or quantitative/evidence pages when relevant, narrative/density rhythm, focal monotony, cross-deck contradiction and generic template substitution.

For decks with six or more analytical pages, the current v7.2 product inspector and pixel-QA gate may hard-block excessive diagram ratio, repeated production fingerprints and shape-only analytical collapse.

## Current pixel boundary
Deterministic metadata cannot claim visual excellence.

- `INTERNAL_CONCEPT_DRAFT` may exist without user-visible pixel certification because it is internal only.
- `USER_VISIBLE_ARTIFACT_DRAFT` **requires** a `PRODUCTION_PAGE_RENDER`, independent actual-pixel PASS tied to its exact hash, closed repair history or first-pass PASS, deck-level actual-output QA where applicable, and exact-file Delivery Certificate.
- `DRAFT_QA_PARTIAL` is internal only and may never be delivered as a user-visible artifact.
- `RELEASE_CANDIDATE` additionally requires current-engagement independent release evidence and parity/proof-index gates.

## Artifact Red Team questions
- Could this page belong to another client if labels change?
- Is the page complex mainly to look sophisticated?
- Did production turn an analytical problem into equal cards because it was easy?
- Does the visual make the claim easier to understand than text alone?
- Can half the elements be removed without losing meaning?
- Are five hypotheses truly different communication approaches?
- Does the selected winner come from evidence/fit rather than candidate position?
- Does the complete deck contain intentional visual rhythm rather than repeated production grammar?

Any failed actual-output review must generate a repair brief and re-render before user-visible delivery.
