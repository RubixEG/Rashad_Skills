| Step 1. GOVERNANCE ARCHITECTURE: Design the governance structure at three levels. (a) Strategic Level - the Innovation Steering Committee: sets programme direction, approves annual budgets and targets, reviews impact reports, makes decisions on strategic programme changes. Define composition, meeting cadence, quorum requirements, and decision authority. (b) Operational Level - the Programme Management function: runs day-to-day programme decisions, manages the implementation partner relationship, reports to the Steering Committee. Define role definitions, reporting lines, and operational decision authority. (c) Advisory Level - the Innovation Advisory Board: provides external expertise, market intelligence, and credibility. Define composition (InsurTech experts, startup investors, international innovation programme leaders), engagement model, and the distinction between advisory and decision authority. |
| Step 2. DECISION RIGHTS MATRIX: Design the explicit decision rights for every significant decision type. Categories: programme strategy decisions (what programmes to run, major design changes), participant selection decisions (who gets into hackathons, accelerators, and incubators), resource allocation decisions (how budget is distributed, when to reallocate), conflict of interest decisions (situations where a governance member has a relationship with a participant), and crisis management decisions (how to respond to programme failures or negative media). For each type: who decides, who is consulted, who is informed, and what the escalation path is.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Step 3. CONFLICT OF INTEREST MANAGEMENT: The regulatory authority both designs the rules of the insurance market and runs programmes that benefit specific market participants. Design the conflict of interest framework: disclosure requirements for all governance participants, the recusal protocol when a governance member has a relationship with a participant, the firewall between regulatory decision-making and programme management, and the public transparency mechanism that demonstrates to the market that the programme is operated impartially.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Step 4. PROGRAMME ACCOUNTABILITY FRAMEWORK: Design how the programme is held accountable for results. The performance management framework for the programme management team (what they are accountable for, how they are evaluated, what happens when targets are missed), the reporting cadence to the Steering Committee and parent authority leadership, and the annual programme review process.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Step 5. REGULATORY SANDBOX GOVERNANCE: Design the governance of the regulatory sandbox pathway the programme enables. Application process, evaluation criteria, decision-making authority (who can approve sandbox status and under what conditions), monitoring requirements during sandbox period, exit conditions (what happens when sandbox period ends), and the escalation path when a sandboxed company wants to transition to full regulatory status.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Step 6. EXTERNAL TRANSPARENCY AND REPORTING: Design the public accountability framework. Annual reports to the market on programme performance, the stakeholder consultation process for programme design changes, the mechanism for programme participants to raise concerns about governance, and alignment with the authority's existing corporate governance and transparency requirements.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
