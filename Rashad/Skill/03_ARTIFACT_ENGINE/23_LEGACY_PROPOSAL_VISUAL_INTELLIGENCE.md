# Legacy Proposal Visual Intelligence — Pattern Library, Not Templates

STATUS: REFERENCE-ONLY KNOWLEDGE EXTRACT
SOURCE CORPUS: historical Rubix proposals across strategy, transformation, market research, events, quality, financial and technical engagements.

## Non-negotiable boundary
Historical proposals may teach **storytelling and information relationships only**. They may not supply current logo, theme, color, fonts, client content, exact layout coordinates, screenshots, contact details, team claims or commercial values.

## Strong patterns to retain as abstract intelligence
1. **Evaluator journey** — connect evaluation criteria to sections/evidence early.
2. **Multi-page methodology narrative** — concept → framework → application → outputs → governance, rather than one overloaded methodology page.
3. **Value-chain / ecosystem maps** for relationship-heavy content.
4. **Current-state → future-state transitions** for transformation logic.
5. **Phase/track roadmaps with gates and outputs** for delivery storytelling.
6. **Governance layers and decision rights** when ownership matters.
7. **Deliverable architecture + acceptance/evidence map** instead of raw BOQ repetition.
8. **Case-study proof rhythm** — context → intervention → outputs → evidence/impact.
9. **Team pages that connect role to workstream/output**, not biography-only pages.
10. **Alternating page rhythm** — dense analytical page → synthesis/decision page → evidence/proof page.
11. **Appendix side-navigation** for long evidence sections, reconstructed in current brand.
12. **Faceted/angled image framing** as a composition idea when it supports the cover/page thesis.

## Weaknesses to avoid
- dark magenta/black full-slide dependency;
- old logos and client identities;
- repeated 4/5-card grids regardless of information relationship;
- decorative hexagons/rings/arrows without analytical meaning;
- tiny text used to fit everything on one page;
- tables used where a dependency/lifecycle/system map is clearer;
- screenshot/certificate walls with no evidence narrative;
- inconsistent magenta variants and uncontrolled accent drift;
- duplicated content across sections;
- long prose before the evaluator takeaway;
- visual novelty that makes RTL reading order ambiguous;
- case-study or capability claims copied from another client.

## Artifact Engine translation rule
Never say “this looks like page X of proposal Y.” Translate the observed strength into a relationship:
`sequence | hierarchy | dependency | ecosystem | comparison | score architecture | evidence chain | lifecycle | governance | roadmap | ownership | portfolio`.
Then construct a new artifact in the current Rubix light palette.

## Benchmark review gate
Before using a legacy-inspired composition, the Artifact Council must answer:
- What relationship does this visualization communicate?
- Would a generic card grid lose meaning?
- Which parts are abstract grammar vs old branding?
- Has every old logo/client/color/asset been removed?
- Is the rebuilt composition stronger in current Rubix identity and RTL?
