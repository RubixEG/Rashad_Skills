# V7 — Consulting Intelligence Brain

**STATUS: CURRENT OVERLAY — PRESERVES V6 RELATIONSHIP/GEOMETRY CAPABILITIES AS DOWNSTREAM TOOLS ONLY**

This module preserves the proven Question → Thesis → Evidence → Relationship reasoning foundation while v7.1 moves the visual-decision authority to the Consulting Artifact Intelligence Brain. V6 relationship/topology/geometry capabilities remain implementation libraries after a communication strategy has earned a relational visual form; they cannot choose the visual strategy or authorize delivery.

## Coordinated reasoning functions
1. **Argument Brain** — management/evaluator question, answer-first thesis, decision and proof burden.
2. **Relationship Brain** — dependency, ownership, control, feedback, threshold, measurement, sequence, risk, cause/effect, comparison, hierarchy, trade-off and constraint.
3. **Communication Strategy Brain** — decides the simplest effective way the answer should be seen; diagram is one option among many.
4. **Information / Exhibit Synthesis Brain** — converts the chosen communication strategy into page-specific information structure, not a reusable template.
5. **Whole-Canvas / Art Direction Brain** — focal point, visual mass, negative space, rhythm, asymmetry, hierarchy and reading path before local geometry.
6. **Executive Compression Brain** — removes redundancy without deleting proof or meaning.
7. **Artifact Skeptic / Simplicity Brain** — attempts to reject generic, decorative, card-dominant, over-complex or decision-useless concepts before production.

## Consulting-grade test
An artifact must make a business answer easier and faster to understand than prose alone. A powerful artifact may be a single number, a table, a chart, a short evidence composition, an image-led page or a complex system map. If the page is only boxes, bullets or decorative arrows, it fails regardless of visual polish.

## Challenge requirement
At least one challenger must articulate a materially plausible alternative interpretation/counterargument for critical pages. The producer must not author the final quality verdict, and user-visible quality must be judged on the actual production pixels.
