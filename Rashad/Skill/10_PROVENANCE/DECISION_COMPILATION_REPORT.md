## Source
`Chatgpt Context(5).md` was treated as behavioral/provenance evidence, not as normal runtime knowledge.

## Method
The long conversation contains repeated proposal iterations, owner corrections, accepted patterns, rejected patterns, temporary experiments, API/Cursor implementation discussions and later supersessions. The mirror therefore does **not** copy every message into runtime.

The compiler model used these categories:
- owner hard/default decisions that remain consistent with v2.5 → `ACTIVE`;
- historical behavior replaced by the current v2.5 authority → `SUPERSEDED`;
- patterns explicitly rejected by the owner/current architecture → `REJECTED`;
- implementation/history useful for rationale only → provenance/reference.

## Supersession safety
The raw transcript may contain old role orders, fixed slide counts and intermediate design/renderer recommendations. Those do not become active merely because they appear later in retrieved text. Current v2.5 authorities remain active unless the owner explicitly overrides them now.

## Why this mirrors the old chat better
The long chat's strength came partly from recent corrections being close to the model. The compiled ledger keeps those **decision outcomes** close to execution without forcing retrieval over the entire transcript.
